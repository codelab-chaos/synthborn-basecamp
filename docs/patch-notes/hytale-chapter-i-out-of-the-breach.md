# It Starts at the Forgotten Temple...

Whether you are playing on an existing Exploration Mode world save or starting a brand new one, players will sense something stirring at the Forgotten Temple. Head there and explore the new Goblin Lab to begin unlocking Chapter I's content.

A new **Chapter** screen clearly lays out your objectives, so you always know what to chase next and what you'll unlock once completing it.

# The Goblin Breach

Once the chapter opens, a temporary portal will appear in the world. It arrives with some force, so watch out! Step through and you’ll find an unstable fragment of Orbis that the goblins have claimed with the Void pressing in around the edges. The space regenerates each time you enter, and the Void will eventually push you out. The pressure is on to get what you need before time runs out. That said, you can earn more time by fighting back the void and upgrading the Goblin Breach Console found within the Goblin Lab.

# New Biome: The Guttering Hollows

Inside you’ll run into predators you can track and trap, Void assassins, treasure chests that spring enemy waves on you, and puzzles. Hunts on the far side of a breach need a Voidheart, and hunt sites now have their own icon on the world map. Bear in mind that we have built this space so we can keep adding to it over time!

Here you will run into majority of the new goblin faction who have been completely revamped, with plenty of shapes, sizes, and tenacious personalities. Some more dangerous than others.

And not every goblin wants a fight! Goblins are wary of the Void's presence and will run from Void creatures unless they have enough friends nearby.

# Scrappy Goblin Gadgets

The goblins brought their toys with them. Admittedly, they are quite scrappy, but this is definitely intentional.

- The **Scrap Drill** chews through a 2x2 patch of blocks at once.
- The **Scrap Glider** lets you take to the skies, dive, and climb while keeping your momentum when its deployed. (Although, mind your Stamina, or the landing gets interesting!)
- **Rocket Boots** give you a double jump.
- The **Hookshot** and its Hook Anchors let you zip across points of the world with ease.
- The **Spyglass** zooms in on whatever is happening in the distance.

Players can also craft the Bangstick which is... well, a stick that goes 'bang!'  
Lastly, make repairs to items without lowering maximum durability with the new Goblin Repair Kit.

# Runestones and Abilities

Chapter I: Out of the Breach introduces a new ability system built on Runestones. Collect them, then slot them into a Runebinder’s Lattice to shape how you fight. Avatars can now equip up to two Ability Runestones, and attach Modifier Runestones to change how they behave.

Abilities cost mana, which regenerates on its own over time, and landing your weapon’s signature ability on an enemy gives a boost.

At launch there are seven Ability Runestones, including Fireball, Ground Slam, Wind Strike, Enrage, Imbue Poison, Chain Hook, and Charged Shot, and eight Modifier Runestones that do things like split a projectile into several, make it bounce between enemies, add a withering or bleeding effect, or trade damage for a faster cooldown. This is only the introduction.

## Prop Hunt

Hide in a crowded tavern, or hunt down the props hiding in it!

As a **Prop**, disguise yourself as random tavern objects like beds, tankards, and barrels, and do your best to blend in. Taunting charges up your escape ability, a smoke bomb paired with a dash, for when things get a little too close. Survive until the end of the match to win.

As a **Hunter**, track down the hidden Props and eliminate them. Every Prop you catch switches sides and joins the hunt. Find them all to win the match!

## Shuffle Jam

A playlist of party-style microgames, shuffled every match. Earn stars in each round, and whoever has the most stars at the end takes the win!

There are 12 microgames at launch. You might be dodging falling blocks in TNT Run one moment and memorizing shapes on the Memory Floor the next.

All 12 Microgames

- **Color Party**: Step onto the dance floor and stand on the correct block to survive!
- **Diving**: Leap off a platform as a frog and claim blocks in the pond.
- **High Ground**: Stay inside the circle to earn points while knocking everyone else out.
- **Lavablocks**: Escape the rising lava by breaking blocks and positioning yourself wisely.
- **Lazers**: Fire deadly lasers at your enemies and rack up the most eliminations.
- **Memory Floor**: Memorize the shapes on the floor and stand on the correct one.
- **Plot Painter**: Paint blocks next to your color and claim the biggest share of the board.
- **Racing**: Shoot the right blocks to speed up and cover the most distance.
- **Stash & Seek**: Hide your relics and find everyone else's.
- **Tile Flip**: Claim tiles by running over them and grab power-ups along the way.
- **TNT Run**: Run and parkour across the map as the blocks fall into the void.
- **TNT Tag**: Tag other players to pass on the TNT before it explodes.

## **Runestones & Abilities**

### **Runestone Ability System**

- Introducing the Runestone Ability system! Craft Runestones and then place them within a Runebinder’s Lattice to modify your combat abilities. One such Runebinder’s Lattice can be found in the Forgotten Temple.

- Runestones will either take the form of an Ability Runestone or a Modifier Runestone. Ability Runestones grant new abilities that come with their own cooldowns and mana costs. Modifier Runestones adjust the behavior, cooldowns, and costs of the Ability Runestone they’re matched with in the Runebinder’s Lattice.

- Modifications to damage, Mana costs, and cooldowns provided by Modifier Runestones are multiplicative with one another.

- Runestones can be crafted using Runestone Shards, which will be found throughout your adventure in Chapter 1. Ability Runestones cost 2 Runestone Shards to craft, and Modifier Runestones cost 1.

- Added seven Ability Runestones, each described in more detail in their own section below:

  - Chain Hook
  - Charged Shot
  - Enrage
  - Fireball
  - Ground Slam
  - Imbue Poison
  - Wind Strike

- Added eight Modifier Runestones, each described in more detail in their own section below:

  - Colossal

  - Enduring

  - Quickening

  - Rebound

  - Rend

  - Splinter

  - Thrift

  - Wither

- The Runebinder’s Lattice will prevent an Ability Runestone from being placed if that Ability Runestone is already active.

- The Runebinder’s Lattice will prevent a Modifier Runestone from being applied to an Ability Runestone if that Modifier Runestone is already active for that ability.

### **Mana & Casting**

- Mana will now be displayed where the Stamina bar was previously.

- Mana passively regenerates over time and is used for all abilities granted by Ability Runestones.

- Signature abilities now restore Mana whenever they hit an enemy. Hitting several enemies at once restores more mana, with diminishing returns.

- Casting an ability granted by an Ability Runestone briefly reduces movement speed by 40%.

### **Ability Runestone: Chain Hook**

- Added the Chain Hook Ability Runestone. This Runestone grants the ability to launch a chain that draws all targets near the point of impact toward you, briefly slowing them.
- Chain Hook deals 3 Bludgeoning damage, costs 50 Mana, and has a cooldown of 6 seconds.
- Chain Hook can be modified with the Rebound, Rend, Splinter, Thrift, and Wither Runestones.

### **Ability Runestone: Charged Shot**

- Added the Charged Shot Ability Runestone. This Runestone grants the ability to channel a bolt of frost that, when released, deals damage and slows the enemy depending on how long it was charged.
- Charged Shot slows the caster more for each stage they’ve channeled, and will automatically fire when fully charged.
- Charged Shot deals 12-58 Water damage, costs 35 Mana, and has a cooldown of 4 seconds.
- Charged Shot can be modified with the Rebound, Rend, Splinter, Thrift, and Wither Runestones.

### **Ability Runestone: Enrage**

- Added the Enrage Ability Runestone. This Runestone grants the ability to channel primal rage, increasing the damage both dealt and received.
- Enraged characters deal 30% more Physical, Elemental, and Projectile damage, but also take 20% more damage from all sources.
- Enrage has a duration of 8 seconds, costs 45 Mana, and has a cooldown of 4 seconds.
- Enrage can be modified with the Enduring, Quickening, and Thrift Runestones.

### **Ability Runestone: Fireball**

- Added the Fireball Ability Runestone. This Runestone grants the ability to launch a flaming ball that explodes on impact.
- Fireball deals 28 Fire damage, costs 30 Mana, and has a cooldown of 2 seconds.
- Fireball can be modified with the Colossal, Quickening, Rebound, Rend, Splinter, Thrift, and Wither Runestones.

### **Ability Runestone: Ground Slam**

- Added the Ground Slam Ability Runestone. This Runestone grants the ability to strike the ground, causing an eruption of stone spikes that can launch foes airborne.
- Ground Slam deals 15-35 Earth damage, costs 40 Mana, and has a cooldown of 5 seconds.
- Ground Slam can be modified with the Colossal, Quickening, Rend, Thrift, and Wither Runestones.

### **Ability Runestone: Imbue Poison**

- Added the Imbue Poison Ability Runestone. This Runestone grants the ability to imbue your weapon with poison.
- Attacks with weapons that are imbued with poison apply a stacking poison to enemies hit. These stacks last 6 seconds each, and up to 3 stacks can be applied at once. Each stack deals 2 Poison damage per second.
- Imbue Poison imbues the weapon for 8 seconds, costs 40 Mana, and has a cooldown of 4 seconds.
- Imbue Poison can be modified with the Enduring, Quickening, and Thrift Runestones.
- Poison is now treated as a subtype of Elemental damage.
- Antidotes now remove poison caused by the Imbue Poison Runestone, and grant a brief immunity to it.

### **Ability Runestone: Wind Strike**

- Added the Wind Strike Ability Runestone. This Runestone grants the ability to empower your next three attacks, causing them to launch piercing arcs of wind.
- The buff provided by Wind Strike that empowers the next three attacks lasts for 8 seconds.
- Wind Strike deals 10 Wind damage, costs 30 Mana, and has a cooldown of 2 seconds.
- Wind Strike can be modified with the Colossal, Enduring, Quickening, Rend, Thrift, and Wither Runestones.

### **Modifier Runestone: Colossal**

- Added the Colossal Modifier Runestone. This Runestone modifies Ability Runestones that affect an area, increasing the damage and size of the area, and slowing projectiles.
- Ability Runestones modified with Colossal deal 40% increased damage, cost 60% more Mana, have a 60% increased area of effect, have a 150% increased projectile size, and have a 50% reduced projectile speed.

### **Modifier Runestone: Enduring**

- Added the Enduring Modifier Runestone. This Runestone modifies Ability Runes, causing their duration to be increased.
- Ability Runestones modified with Enduring cost 30% more Mana, have a 60% increased Cooldown, and last 50% longer.

### **Modifier Runestone: Quickening**

- Added a new Quickening Modifier Runestone. This Runestone modifies Ability Runes, reducing their damage, but making them usable more often, and faster.
- Ability Runestones modified with Quickening deal 25% less damage, cost 20% less Mana, have a 30% reduced Cooldown, expire 20% faster, and move 40% faster.

### **Modifier Runestone: Rebound**

- Added a new Rebound Modifier Runestone. This Runestone modifies Ability Runes, causing projectiles fired to bounce off of enemies.
- Projectiles have their damage reduced by 20% each time they rebound off an enemy.
- Ability Runestones modified with Rebound cost 25% more Mana, have a 30% increased Cooldown, rebound 2 additional times, and move 40% faster.

### **Modifier Runestone: Rend**

- Added a new Rend Modifier Runestone. This Runestone modifies Ability Runestones, causing enemies to bleed for a duration, taking damage over time and receiving increased physical damage.
- Enemies affected by the Rend debuff take 2 Physical damage per second for 6 seconds, and receive 15% more Physical damage.
- Ability Runestones modified with Rend deal 20% less damage, cost 20% more Mana, and have a 25% increased Cooldown.

### **Modifier Runestone: Splinter**

- Added the Splinter Modifier Runestone. This Runestone modifies Ability Runes, causing projectiles fired to be split into additional projectiles. These additional projectiles cannot splinter again.
- Projectiles have their damage reduced by 30% each time they splinter from an enemy.
- Splintered projectiles have 30% reduced projectile and area of effect size.
- Ability Runestones modified with Splinter cost 25% more Mana, have a 20% increased Cooldown, and splinter 2 additional times.

### **Modifier Runestone: Thrift**

- Added a new Thrift Modifier Runestone. This Runestone modifies Ability Runes, causing them to cost less Mana, but also deal less damage.
- Ability Runestones modified with Thrift deal 20% less damage, and cost 25% less Mana.

### **Modifier Runestone: Wither**

- Added a new Wither Modifier Runestone. This Runestone modifies Ability Runes, causing them to apply the Wither debuff. This debuff slows the afflicted enemy and causes them to deal less damage.
- The Wither debuff lasts for 2 seconds.
- Enemies affected by the Wither debuff deal 15% less Physical, Elemental, and Projectile damage, and have their movement speed reduced by 10%.
- Ability Runestones modified with Wither deal 30% less damage, cost 20% more Mana, and have a 30% increased Cooldown.

## **Combat & Movement**

### **Food & Potions**

- Health potions now share a 15-second cooldown. Their descriptions have been updated.

- Stamina potions now share a 30-second cooldown. Their descriptions have been updated.

- Taking damage will no longer interrupt consuming food or potions.

- Health, Stamina, and Energy Potions now stack up to 10.

- Food now takes 2.5 seconds to consume (previously 2-3 seconds, depending on the type).

- Bread, Popcorn, and Cheese now restore 5% Health (previously 15%).

- Consuming raw fruit, vegetables, or crops will now grant a minor Health regeneration buff for 1 minute. Raw meat, fish, and eggs do not grant this buff.

- Movement speed will now be reduced while consuming food or potions by 25% (previously 60%).

- Buffs granted from consuming cooked food will now last 6 minutes. This affects Health regeneration, maximum Health, and maximum Stamina buffs that various cooked foods can grant.

- Updated the animations when consuming various foods, from raw meats to cooked vegetables.

### **Weapons**

- Iron Crossbow combo shot damage has been reduced from 27 to 13.
- Ancient Steel Crossbow standard shot damage has been reduced from 16 to 10.5, the combo shot damage has been reduced from 42 to 14, and the Signature Attack damage has been reduced from 123 to 82.
- Sword, dagger, mace, and battleaxe attacks are now roughly 15% faster.
- Sword hits will now cause less vertical knockback.
- Shields can no longer be raised to cause the animation of an attack to be interrupted and end early, after damage could have been dealt. They can still be used to interrupt the wind-up of an attack, before damage is dealt.
- Swapping hotbar slots will no longer allow attacks to be executed sooner than intended after performing a sword, dagger, mace or battleaxe attack.
- Dagger attacks now check line of sight like other weapons, so they can no longer damage enemies through walls.

### **Movement Speed during Attacks**

- Attacking with a crossbow will now reduce movement speed during the attack by 50% (previously 0%).
- Attacking with a sword, mace, or battleaxe will now reduce movement speed during the attack by 25% (previously 0-40% depending on the attack).
- Attacking with daggers will now reduce movement speed during the attack by 15% (previously 0-50% depending on the attack).

### **Flinching, Blocking & Knockback**

- Non-Elite enemies you encounter in areas related to Chapter 1 will flinch when hurt, interrupting their attacks. If an enemy is made to flinch too many times in a short duration, they will temporarily become immune to further flinching.

- It is no longer possible to block the damage being dealt by damage over time debuffs.

- In areas where PvP is disabled, player attacks will no longer knock other players backwards.

- In areas where spawn protection is in place, players will be unaffected by knockback.

### **Weapon & Tool Durability**

- Breaking blocks using a tool in the utility slot will now correctly reduce that tool’s durability.
- Tools that use the per-block durability loss mode will now lose durability for each block.
- The durability of Crude, Bronze, Steel, and Frost weaponry has been increased from 80 to 120.
- The durability of Copper weaponry has been increased from 100 to 200.
- The durability of Iron weaponry has been increased from 120 to 250.
- The durability of Cobalt and Thorium weaponry has been increased from 150 to 350.
- The durability of Adamantite and Ancient Steel weaponry has been increased from 180 to 500.
- The durability of Crude pickaxes and hatchets has been increased from 150 to 200.
- The durability of Copper pickaxes and hatchets has been increased from 200 to 300.
- The durability of Iron pickaxes and hatchets has been increased from 250 to 500.
- The durability of Cobalt and Thorium pickaxes and hatchets has been increased from 325 to 700.
- The durability of Adamantite pickaxes and hatchets has been increased from 400 to 1000.

### **Movement & Collision**

- Mantling is now possible when standing atop a half-slab or horizontal beam.

- Projectile impacts will no longer lose their visual and sound effect prematurely.

- Adjustments to the horizontal movement speed from entity effects are now applied when in the air, swimming, climbing, or rolling.

- Getting stuck inside blocks will no longer push players through thin walls. Players will instead be nudged back into open space, or held in place if no open space is found.

- Smaller characters can no longer phase through ceilings by mantling.

- Walking down stairs will no longer briefly place players into a falling state.

- The Stamina warning will now appear when attempting to guard with a shield without having sufficient Stamina to do so.

- NPCs will now successfully find solid ground in worlds where the terrain sits outside of the old fixed height range.
- Entities that have been scaled in size will now have their hitbox scale with them again.
- Larger creatures will now spawn more reliably with spawn markers.

## **Creative Tools**

### **Node Editor**

- The Node Editor now opens to display a start screen, where you can pick what to create or jump straight back into a recent file.
- The Node Editor now reopens on the workspace type you used last.
- Files dropped onto the Node Editor will now open directly, on Windows and macOS.
- The Node Editor now has a light theme and a dark theme. Select the one you prefer by going to File \> Preferences.
- Added a graph minimap, toggled from the View menu. It frames your current viewport, highlights your selection, and clicking anywhere on it jumps you to that part of the graph.
- Dragging nodes now snaps them into line with their neighbors and to a grid, with guide lines showing what they are lining up against. Multi-node selections keep their internal alignments as they’re dragged.
- The \`ManualCurve\` and \`FunctionForY\` nodes now have an interactive curve graph. Right-click the plot to add a point, press Delete or Backspace to remove one, and points can cross each other or move outside of the visible range without the plot locking up. Panning and zooming the canvas no longer interferes with editing.
- Curve values snap to whole numbers as they’re dragged. Hold Alt for a fractional value.
- Material fields now use a searchable asset picker, with a texture thumbnail rendered for each material in the picker itself.
- Added a Trig Density node to the WorldGen workspace.
- Added an option to keep undoing for as long as Ctrl+Z is held down. This is switched off by default.
- The Node Editor now provides a clearer error message when it cannot read a file.
- Brown and Bright Red are now available as node colors.
- Opening and closing a large graph will no longer cause the editor to freeze, and files load in the background so the editor remains responsive.
- Fixed a number of crashes and inconsistent states when switching between workspace types.
- Fixed a memory leak where every graph opened stayed in memory for the rest of the session.
- Fixed a node type the editor does not recognize losing information when a graph was saved.
- Copying and pasting nodes no longer loses the connections between them.
- Shift-clicking to select nodes now works reliably.
- Fixed a number of visual issues related to group titles.
- Fixed a number of startup and stability problems.
- The Asset Node Editor in Creative Tools now opens on Linux.

### **Builder Tools**

- Builder tools will now be deactivated when leaving a world.
- The map now refreshes across the whole area when you paste a prefab or a clipboard with the builder tools, instead of only the chunk under your cursor. Undoing and redoing an edit refreshes the map for the area it changes as well.
- Builder tools now work at any height in cubic worlds.
- The height limit has been removed when copying a selection.
- Edits that are too large for a creative tool to perform in one action will no longer attempt the edit and will now notify the editor what size would fit.
- Attempting to save a prefab wider than the creative tools can hold will now fail instead of saving only part of the prefab.
- Undoing and redoing will now result in a message saying how many steps were undone or redone.
- Fixed table flipping. Flipping a selection of blocks that includes furniture will no longer cause issues such as invisible hitboxes being left behind.
- Fixed counts that read wrong when only one block, chunk, or step was involved.
- Fixed a rendering crash that could occur when using certain builder tools.

### **Trigger Volumes**

- Trigger volumes can now set your respawn point, move a world's spawn, or clear your respawn points.
- A Tag Condition on a trigger volume can now compare one tag against another tag instead of only against a fixed value.
- The Show Event Title trigger volume effect now has a Style option in place of the old "Is Major" toggle, so you pick the look of the title directly. Its field labels also show correctly again.
- Added an Activate Trigger Volumes toggle to the creative mode quick settings. With it switched off, you can walk through your own trigger volumes without setting them off.
- Trigger volumes set to apply to the source of projectiles will now correctly do so, rather than considering the projectile as its own source.
- Play Sound settings on Trigger Volumes now show proper Volume and Pitch labels rather than raw text ids.
- Sounds played by Trigger Volumes will no longer play far louder than intended.
- Trigger volume timers will now be affected by the speed of time within the world. This means commands such as /time pause or /time dilation will be respected by trigger volume timers.
- Timed Trigger Volume events will no longer sneakily count down in paused singleplayer worlds, only to all fire at once when the game is unpaused.
